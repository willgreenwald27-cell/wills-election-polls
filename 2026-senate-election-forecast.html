What the interactive 2026 Senate map shows

The 2026 Senate Prediction map presents Will’s independent calls across the country. Select a state for candidate information, party outlooks, predictions, and other available polling details. Race categories help visitors distinguish relatively safe contests from more competitive races.

The interactive dashboard also summarizes the projected Senate balance of power. Because forecasts and polling data can change, the interactive page is the source to consult for the newest available update, rather than relying on figures reproduced in this guide.

Competitive 2026 Senate races to follow

Visitors interested in close Senate elections can explore states such as Maine, Texas, Ohio, Alaska, North Carolina, and Georgia through the state map. Each race has different candidates, polling context, and political dynamics. Comparing the state-level analysis makes it easier to see where an independent prediction agrees with—or departs from—the latest listed polling.

How to read the predictions responsibly

A forecast is an assessment of an uncertain future, not a guarantee. It should be evaluated using the evidence and assumptions behind it. A polling lead is not necessarily a forecasted victory, and a historical polling miss does not establish that polls will miss in the same direction again.

Will’s website presents original judgments alongside election and polling information. Visitors should distinguish an analyst’s prediction from a poll result or a formally calibrated statistical probability model.

What happens after November 3, 2026?

Election results provide an opportunity to evaluate calls against what happened. The website’s Polls vs. Reality research explores historical polling misses as context for interpreting forecasts. Preserving dated predictions and explaining changes makes any election forecasting project more useful to readers.

Forecasts are independent commentary, not official election results or polling releases. Visit the interactive map for the site's most recent published analysis.
